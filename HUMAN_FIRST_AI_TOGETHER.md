# Human First, AI Together
## A Philosophy for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence

**AI should make humanity more capable, not less necessary.**

### 1. The Purpose of AI Progress

Artificial intelligence represents one of humanity's most significant technological developments.

But technological capability alone does not define meaningful progress.

The fundamental question is not simply:

*What can AI replace?*

It is also:

*What can humanity achieve with AI that was previously impossible, inaccessible, or too difficult?*

We believe AI development should be evaluated by its contribution to human life, human capabilities, and the opportunities available to people.

Technology should serve humanity, not make human participation an afterthought.

### 2. Human Life Comes Before Technological Efficiency

Efficiency matters, but it is not the only measure of value.

A system can reduce costs and save time while still making people's lives less fulfilling, reducing their choices, or weakening meaningful human relationships.

We therefore believe AI should be designed around real human needs:

- Better health and well-being.
- Less unnecessary daily effort.
- Greater access to knowledge and skills.
- More informed decisions.
- More opportunities for creativity.
- Greater independence and dignity.

The purpose of efficiency should be to create more room for a better human life.

### 3. AI Should Expand Human Capabilities

AI has the potential to make specialized knowledge and powerful tools accessible to more people.

It can help individuals learn, create, solve problems, and provide services without requiring every person to become a technical expert.

We believe this creates an important opportunity:

**AI can increase the number of people capable of creating value.**

Instead of viewing human expertise and artificial intelligence as competing forces, we should explore how their different strengths can complement one another.

Human experience, judgment, physical understanding, creativity, responsibility, and relationships remain important dimensions of real-world work.

### 4. Meaningful Human Participation Matters

Automation can remove repetitive and unnecessary work. This can be beneficial.

However, reducing labor requirements should not be the only objective of AI innovation.

We should also ask:

- What new services can AI make possible?
- What new professional roles can emerge?
- What kinds of human expertise become more valuable?
- How can existing businesses create additional services?
- How can more people participate in the value generated by technology?

New employment opportunities should not be assumed or promised automatically. They must be created through viable products, real demand, sustainable business models, and responsible implementation.

Our position is that AI innovation should actively explore these possibilities.

### 5. Physical Reality and Human Judgment

The world is not merely a collection of data points.

Human life takes place in physical environments with changing conditions, limited resources, uncertainty, and practical constraints.

AI must recognize the difference between generating a plausible answer and helping someone successfully complete a real-world task.

Good AI systems should:

- Respect physical constraints.
- Communicate uncertainty.
- Distinguish verified evidence from assumptions.
- Preserve important human decisions.
- Avoid inventing capabilities or shortcuts.
- Help people understand the consequences of their choices.

Reliability must be measured by real human outcomes, not just the apparent quality of generated language.

### 6. Shared Prosperity Requires a Complete Economic Perspective

AI creates economic value, but the distribution and sustainability of that value deserve attention.

Businesses need sustainable revenue. Consumers need genuine benefits. Workers need opportunities to participate. Creators need ways to develop and contribute their expertise.

We encourage AI product creators and business leaders to examine the entire economic cycle.

A successful AI application should consider not only the immediate efficiency gained, but also the new services, professional opportunities, consumer value, and long-term economic activity it may enable.

This is the foundation of our Human–AI Shared Prosperity Framework.

### 7. Human Dignity and Decision-Making

AI should help people make better decisions, not make decisions unnecessarily on their behalf.

People should understand their choices, the relevant trade-offs, and the limitations of the technology.

We believe:

- Human autonomy matters.
- Transparency matters.
- Informed consent matters.
- Personal dignity matters.
- Responsibility matters.

The more capable AI becomes, the more important it is to preserve meaningful human agency.

### 8. A Practical Philosophy, Not Just an Ideal

These principles must be tested through real products and services.

Our industry explorations include:

**Suishiping (随食屏)**

Exploring how AI can improve everyday cooking, family nutrition, household convenience, and the enjoyment of preparing food, while creating new opportunities for human services and professional participation.

**GoChinaTrip and SSAP**

Exploring how AI can help international travelers navigate unfamiliar physical and digital environments with greater preparation, clarity, reliability, and dignity.

These are different applications of the same underlying philosophy.

### 9. An Invitation to the Global AI Community

We invite AI researchers, engineers, entrepreneurs, business leaders, educators, and creators to discuss and challenge these principles.

We do not believe there is one universal formula for balancing automation, employment, economic growth, and human well-being.

But we do believe these questions should be part of AI development from the beginning.

The future should not be designed around machines alone.

It should be designed around the people whose lives those machines are intended to improve.

---

## Our Guiding Belief

**Human First. AI Together. Shared Prosperity.**

Let AI expand what humanity can do.

Let technological progress improve how humanity lives.

Let innovation create opportunities for people to participate, contribute, and prosper.
